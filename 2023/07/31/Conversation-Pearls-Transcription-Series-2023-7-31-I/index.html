Conversation Pearls Transcription Series - 2023.7.31 - I

High Throughput Multiplex PCR

Is PJ considered a leading company in China specializing in high-throughput multiplex PCR? There is speculation that it possesses the significant potential and maybe a notable competitor to AmoyDx in the future.

PJ’s high-throughput multiplex PCR technology is not innovative. This technology has been available for a considerable time. I started to mention the technology was through a startup, named “BY Medical” in China, where the concept of targeted NGS and high-throughput multiplex PCR was still intermingled and not fully distinguished. It is worth mentioning that PJ is among the earliest companies, perhaps the only one, to develop and manufacture the physical machine for this purpose in China.

NGS destroyer? It could be used to detect copy number variation (CNV), loss of heterozygosity (LOH), homozygous deletion (HD), and homologous recombination deficiency (HRD), which common PCR is not feasible in these scenarios.

Essentially, it may be called a high throughput multiplex PCR, with a theoretical throughput of 10,000 targets. However, in practice, it remains an ordinary multiplex PCR since only a limited number of targets, including several kinds of mutations, can be clinically utilized, typically ranging from a few to several dozen. Therefore, the application of such high target numbers PCR reaction, like 10,000, is not relevant in a clinical setting.

In the field of oncology, ordinary multiplex PCR is not feasible and will continue to rely on NGS platforms. Switching to ultra-multiplex PCR is not possible due to industry ecological challenges. However, in the field of infectious diseases, both multiplex and ultra-multiplex PCR are being extensively used as they outperform NGS in terms of performance. There is an urgent need for alternative methods in the field of infectious diseases due to the limitations of NGS.

I have looked at their brochure, which said a single fluorescent dye can perform 10-fold PCR. With two dyes, it can go up to 100. Therefore, I want to know if their promotion aligns with their actual performance.

The key is fluorescent coding. According to the manufacturer’s introduction, each fluorescent has 10 gradient levels, which is a decimal encoding. The theoretical upper limit is 100 with two lanes, and 1000 with three, similar to dividing the values of the melting curve into 10 intervals. Theoretically, it’s feasible, but whether it can be achieved in design and stably perform a 100-fold PCR reaction, is hard to say.

Although it is referred to as a 100-fold, it may be done with fewer folds. It is impossible to fail with a dozen or so folds, especially in the field of infectious diseases, since the technology is highly mature and well-established. For example, in the case of the melting curve, one segment can be used to identify 30 to 40 types of mutations, so how could it fail?

Another aspect is the definition of “folds.” Does it refer to one fold for a gene sequence or one fold for a mutation? These are just playing with words. It doesn’t matter how it’s promoted; the essence remains the same, and clinical demand is the core. I would call it the first principle of medicine. No matter how much you boast, it is nothing without demand.
