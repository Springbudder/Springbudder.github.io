Mass Spectrometry, MALDI-TOF MS, and NGS

Question 1: What are the advantages of MALDI-TOF MS?

Nucleotide MALDI-TOF MS is, by its very nature, a means of detecting the products of multiplex PCR. If you want to analyze the advantages of it, take it in two ways:

  1. What are the advantages of multiplex PCR?
  2. What are the advantages of mass spectrometry over other product analysis techniques?

To use a car analogy, nucleotide MALDI-TOF MS is like an electric semi-trailer. The problem becomes twofold: first, comparing the semi-trailer to other kinds of vehicles in transportation; and second, analyzing the advantages of an electric head versus a hybrid or fuel.

You always infinitely devalue what you can touch, or have around you, and elevate the mirage in the distance. Unbeknownst to you, the mirage is considered a pile of shit by others, such as yourself.

Question 2: What is the future vision of Mass Spectrometry?

The field of medicine is hungry for innovative products, but the criteria for judging them are harsh, which leads to a background of extreme conservatism in medicine itself. Relatively radical methods in other fields are completely inaccessible to medicine because they are unstable, inconvenient, expensive, etc. The methods that can be used in medicine are those that are well-established and have an acceptable cost profile. Was NGS first applied in medicine? No. Environmental microbiologists used it first. Were chromatography and mass spectrometry first used in medicine? No, they were used by people in chemistry.

Why does mass spectrometry have a big future? Because in other fields, mass spectrometry has matured beyond maturity, doing chemistry labs, playing mass spectrometry is about the same as I weigh on the scale, so common, and is an irreplaceable method of trace material detection, this maturity penetration into the field of medicine is inevitable, but the medical detection of mass spectrometry is still in the very early stages, many large hospitals have just begun to purchase the mass spectrometry platform, the previous is the chromatography, or even older than chromatography, chemical chromatography platform. Many large tertiary hospitals have just started to purchase mass spectrometry platforms.

The slow development of mass spectrometry in the medical field is because there is not so much demand for biomarker detection, and the precision requirements are not so high. This is due to the conservative nature of medicine, and the vast amount of biomarker research, but very little of it has reached the clinic, which is the nature of the fruitful desert in the field of biomarker exploration.

So, if you’re going into a career where biomarker discovery work is explored, it’s destined to be a path where you pay a lot but get little in return.

Question 3: What is the future vision of nucleotide MALDI-TOF MS and its counterpart technology?

I led the expert consensus on the first nucleic acid mass spectrometry product, and I know the advantages and disadvantages best. Mass spectrometry is a great platform, the current obstacle is cost, and the long-term obstacle is the progress of biomarker research. This is the big mass spectrometry level, The future is bright, but the road is long, because the cost relative to the current chemiluminescence, immunofluorescence, and whatnot, or too high, and there is not much room to shrink in the future. Looking at genetic testing alone and the development of nucleotide MALDI-TOF MS and NGS, nucleotide MALDI-TOF MS will precipitate into the clinic, NGS will shrink back to the lab, and the future is clear-cut. Agena is just now realizing this and has already begun to change. NGS is transitioning to a small panel, and targeted NGS is also a retrenchment strategy, but it won’t last long, and when the current batch of NGS machines is retired, targeted NGS will be fast-forwarded straight to obsolescence in the clinical applications.

Question 4: What is the future of genetic testing?

The entire future of genetic testing is bleak. Genetic testing is a very, very small piece of the overall healthcare industry and was born with the original sin of commercial bribery and medical corruption. Genetic testing is an element of the gray zone except for definitive tests for genetic diseases (such as thalassemia), whether it be cancer or infectious diseases. Even if all genetic testing disappeared now, it wouldn’t hurt the medical industry. So-called precision medicine, or nucleic acid testing, is the icing on the cake, not the snow.
