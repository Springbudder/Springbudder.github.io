#### Companion Diagnostics and Pharmaceutical Industry



> “伴随诊断产品，产品因与药物使用直接相关，应有充分药物疗效、数据，暂不建议纳入LDT试点品种范围。”
>
> I don't understand what that means. 

It means that the companion diagnostics are not eligible for Shanghai's LDT pilot application and that if these diagnostics are to be carried out legally and in a compliant manner, they should be declared for Class III medical device registration in accordance with standard procedures.



> Are foreign companion diagnostics categorized as IVD or LDT?

Companion diagnostics are required to be regulated in accordance with IVD In China and other countries, but LDT is also applied in clinical practice widely, so it is hard to define whether it belongs to IVD or LDT.



> “因与药物使用直接相关，应有充分药物疗效、数据。”

> What's the point of this statement?

Additional clinical data are needed, or national and international guideline recommendations.



> Oh. That is, the drug needs to be recommended in clinical guidelines before we can develop the associated companion diagnostic reagents? It makes sense that a drug that is not in the clinical guidelines will not be approved by clinical and regulatory authorities for companion diagnostics

What's the point of doing companion diagnostics when your drug isn't even mentioned in the clinical guidelines?



> I understand. So, basically, not all drugs are necessarily mentioned or recommended in clinical guidelines. However, our companion diagnostics are submitted by drug companies as part of their drug submissions, and they are not related to clinical guidelines. It's worth noting that clinical guidelines usually focus on the most commonly used drugs, and they don't necessarily cover or recommend all the drugs available out there.

Submitting companion diagnostics with drugs is a relatively new practice. The reputable drug companies that produce effective medications are usually included in the clinical guidelines. On the other hand, drugs that are not included in the guidelines are generally considered not very good. It's hard to imagine an effective drug not being included in the guidelines because that would be counterproductive to the interests of both the medical and marketing departments. But, there are always challenges involved in communicating with KOLs and getting inclusion in the guidelines.

It's worth noting that there is a bit of a conflict between the interests of pharmaceutical companies and the development of complementary diagnostic tools. Companion diagnostics are intended to help make a drug more effective, but they may also limit the number of patients who can benefit from the medication. So, in a way, companion diagnostics are often developed to compensate for a failed drug development process or to achieve significance in clinical data analysis through patient grouping.



> Is companion diagnostics a mandatory incidental parameter of a drug?

Of course not.



> So, if I understand correctly, companion diagnostic trials are experiments that drug companies are required to conduct to show that a drug is effective, right? It seems like drug companies wouldn't bother developing companion diagnostics if the new drug already worked well for different groups of people. Speaking of which, do metabolic enzymes and similar things qualify as companion diagnostics? By the way, have you ever wondered why cold and flu medicines don't have companion diagnostics? I know I have so many questions without answers!



Metabolic enzymes are not considered companion diagnostics since they are typically tested in vitro and cell culture, rather than through gene sequencing. As for cold remedies, they don't require companion diagnostics because they tend to work immediately upon consumption. In other words, if a medication is already effective for most people, why would a drug company want to spend more money on additional testing? It's important to think about these issues on a higher level to fully understand them.
