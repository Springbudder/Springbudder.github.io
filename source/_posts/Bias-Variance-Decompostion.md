For the test sample $x$, $y\_{D}$ is the unitary marker in the dataset, and $y$ is the real marker for $x$, $f(x, D)$ is the predict output generated by model $f$ based on the training dataset $D$.
Take the regression task as an instance, the prediction expectation of model is $$\overline{f}(x) = E\_{D}[f(x;D)]$$When the different training dataset with the same size was applied, the variance should be $$var(x) = E\_{D}[(f(x;D) - \overline{f}(x))^{2}]$$The noise would be $$\epsilon^{2} = E\_{D}[(y\_{D}-y)^{2}]$$The difference between expected output and the real marker is called <b>bias</b>, i.e.$$bias^{2}(x) = (\overline{f}(x)-y)^{2}$$Assume the expectation of noise is $0$, $E\_{D}[y\_{D}-y] = 0$. We can decompose the generalization error of trained model use polynomial calculation
\begin{equation}
\begin{split} 
E(f;D)& = E\_{D}[(f(x;D)-y\_{D})^{2}]\\\
& =E\_{D}[(f(x;D) - \overline{f}(x) + \overline{f}(x) - y\_{D})^{2}]\\\ 
& =E\_{D}[(f(x;D) - \overline{f}(x))^{2}] + E\_{D}[\overline{f}(x) - y\_{D}]^{2}\\\ 
& \quad +E\_{D}[2(f(x;D) - \overline{f}(x))(\overline{f}(x)-y\_{D})]\\\ 
& =E\_{D}[(f(x;D)-\overline{f}(x))^{2}] + E\_{D}[(\overline{f}(x) - y\_{D})^{2}]\\\
& =E\_{D}[(f(x;D)-\overline{f}(x))^{2}] + E\_{D}[(\overline{f}(x) - y + y - y\_{D})^{2}]\\\
& =E\_{D}[(f(x;D)-\overline{f}(x))^{2}] + E\_{D}[(\overline{f}(x) - y)^{2}] + E\_{D}[(y - y\_{D})^{2}]\\\
& \quad +2E\_{D}[(\overline{f}(x) - y)(y - y\_{D})]\\\
& =E\_{D}[(f(x;D)-\overline{f}(x))^{2}] + (\overline{f}(x) - y)^{2} + E\_{D}[(y - y\_{D})^{2}]\\\
\end{split} 
\end{equation}Hence we can draw a conclusion that $$E(f;D) = bias^{2}(x) + var(x) + \epsilon^{2}$$In other words, generalization error could be comprehensed as the sum of bias, viarance and noise.
